## Background
So why do we need shredders? 
Shredders play a critical role in modern material management because they change the physical scale of waste, making it usable again. At a basic level, shredding reduces size. Large, bulky objects such as plastic bottles, failed 3D prints, packaging, and scrap parts take up significant space. By cutting them into small, uniform pieces, shredders reduce storage volume and make transport more efficient and cost-effective. This simple reduction in size has major logistical and economic benefits. Beyond volume reduction, shredders are essential to recycling systems. Most recycling processes require material in small, consistent fragments before it can be washed, sorted, melted, or reprocessed. Large solid objects cannot be fed directly into extruders or melting systems. Shredding converts irregular waste into flakes or chips that can flow, heat evenly, and be transformed into pellets or filament. Without this step, closed-loop recycling would be inefficient or impossible. Shredders also improve material consistency. Smaller particles expose more surface area, which allows for better cleaning and more uniform melting. This leads to higher-quality recycled products. In industrial settings, controlled particle size is necessary to ensure predictable mechanical properties and reliable downstream processing. In decentralized or small-scale manufacturing systems, shredders enable local recycling. Instead of sending plastic waste to distant facilities, individuals or small workshops can process their own scrap into usable feedstock. This reduces waste, lowers material costs, and supports circular production models.
